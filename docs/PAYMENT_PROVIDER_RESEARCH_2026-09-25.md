# UK payment-provider research — 25 September 2026

Research commissioned for The Loyalty Loop and checked alongside its existing Fidel implementation. This is a research recommendation, not approval to build, enrol merchants, process payments or change the confirmed spend-based product rules. No provider registration, contact, settings change or transaction was performed. Separate cloud task: **Payment Integration Comparison** (`6ab64010-51d0-83eb-9779-7b5010a9639c`).

**Review corrections:** Fidel transaction identity uses the verified nested `card.id` contract in this project. The cloud draft's `cardId` wording has been corrected below. Independent follow-up also found published free API access for Square and Zettle, plus a material Square UK terms restriction; those findings supersede the draft's overly broad pricing uncertainty. For launch eligibility, see the independent implementation review; current Fidel Test-mode auth/clearing evidence does not establish genuine refund delivery or a safe live launch.

## Plain English conclusion

The Loyalty Loop can read useful merchant payment data from these providers, but **a payment feed is not automatically a shopper identification system**. Fidel is the clearest documented route for linking a shopper to purchases across participating merchants. Square is the strongest candidate for a separate, merchant specific automatic earning pilot because its payment records document a card fingerprint. That fingerprint cannot be treated as Fidel’s card ID, shared across different Square sellers, or assumed to survive a switch between a physical card and a phone wallet. SumUp Solo and Zettle can supply purchase information, but the documented transaction fields reviewed here do not establish a dependable way to recognise a returning shopper.

**Research recommendation (not an approved product change):** keep spend based rewards at every shop. Enable automatic earning only for a payment route whose shopper match and refund handling have passed a live test. Offer manual spend entry or verification everywhere else.

## Detailed findings

### SumUp and the available Solo

**Verified.** SumUp documents OAuth for integrations authorised by other merchants, with `transactions.read` or `transactions.history` for transaction access. Its transaction endpoints list a merchant’s history and provide payment status, payment type, entry mode, transaction ID, refund information and card brand and last four. The Solo Cloud API is a different capability: an integration starts the checkout on a paired Solo, supplies an Affiliate Key, and can receive `solo.transaction.updated` results. This documentation does **not** prove that an ordinary sale started on the standalone Solo interface sends that particular Cloud API webhook to an unrelated loyalty app. The transaction history endpoint is the relevant route to assess for those sales.

**Identity conclusion.** Last four and brand can coincide for different people. The documented transaction card response does not provide a card identifier that I can verify as reusable across purchases, merchant accounts, replacement cards or Apple Pay and Google Pay. SumUp’s `id`, `transaction_code` and client supplied transaction identifiers identify a **transaction**, not a returning cardholder. None should be used to enrol a shopper automatically.

**Events, refunds and tests.** Cloud API describes real time transaction results for Cloud API initiated Solo checkouts. General SumUp webhooks document checkout status changes; I could not verify a general push notification covering *every* independently initiated Solo sale, or a delivery latency and retry guarantee suitable for that claim. Transaction details and history expose refunds, including partial refund amounts and events, so polling and reconciliation are necessary even if a webhook is used. SumUp provides a sandbox merchant and Virtual Solo for pairing and checkout flows; Virtual Solo cannot simulate a physical tap, real funds or offline payments. A physical Solo test is therefore necessary for the user’s actual checkout path.

**Commercial status.** The Affiliate Key is required for the card present Cloud API route and may carry agreed partner terms. Public documentation examined here does not establish a price, an unrestricted right to repurpose merchant transaction data for independent loyalty, or that approval for any necessary partner arrangement is automatic. Treat those as open contractual questions, not implementation assumptions.

### Square

**Verified.** Square seller OAuth grants scoped access. With `PAYMENTS_READ`, `payment.created` and `payment.updated` notifications cover sales made in Square Point of Sale and Square Terminal, as well as API payments. A completed sale must be checked for completed status before crediting spend. Square says webhook notifications are typically sent within 60 seconds, requires acknowledgement within 10 seconds, and retries failed deliveries for up to 24 hours. Those are documented delivery characteristics, not a guaranteed time to award points.

**Identity conclusion.** Square’s `card_details.card.fingerprint` is a useful candidate for recognising repeated payments **within its documented scope**. Square describes the Card fingerprint as based on the card number and intended to work across locations within an application; Square staff say fingerprints differ between sellers. Staff also explain that Apple Pay and Google Pay present a different payment card number, yielding a different fingerprint from the underlying physical card. A replacement card, a different wallet or device, and cross provider matching therefore require separate enrolment or verification. A Square customer ID or stored card is also not proof that every later walk in terminal payment will carry that customer ID.

**First link.** The safer pilot flow is a shopper signed into The Loyalty Loop, a merchant assisted first purchase, and an explicit confirmation that its **specific Square payment** belongs to that shopper. Record its fingerprint only after checking the merchant, amount, time and payment ID and preventing competing claims. Subsequent matching is restricted to the verified seller and credential type. This is a **proposed inference and control**, not a Square provided shopper consent or linking feature. Square also supports storing a card against a customer through its Cards API, but its card on file capability is primarily for later payments and should not be introduced just to infer ownership of unrelated terminal sales.

**Refunds and tests.** Subscribe to refund events and reconcile refund objects against their original Square payment ID; adjust earned spend by the actual refunded amount, including partial refunds. Square Sandbox supports API payments and webhooks but not card-present payments on physical hardware. A real UK merchant and device must establish fingerprint presence and consistency on the intended terminal, card and wallet combinations. API access is advertised as free; rate limits, optional product fees and the UK terms restriction above remain separate considerations. Sources: [Refunds API](https://developer.squareup.com/docs/refunds-api/overview), [Sandbox payments](https://developer.squareup.com/docs/devtools/sandbox/payments).

### Zettle and PayPal POS

**Verified.** Zettle documents UK API availability, OAuth based merchant authorisation, a Purchase API, and Pusher `PurchaseCreated` events. A purchase can include its source, time, amount, products and payment methods; the published example includes `maskedPan`. Pusher requires a responsive HTTPS endpoint and documents a 10 second request timeout. I did not find a substantiated end to end latency guarantee, retry schedule or stable card identity field for purchases in the primary documentation reviewed. A masked PAN must not be promoted into a shopper key.

**Scope and testing.** Zettle distinguishes partner-hosted integrations from merchant-hosted apps. Its FAQ confirms UK API availability, free API access and no general API sandbox; Android SDK developer mode offers simulated payment/refund responses. That SDK mode does not establish what an ordinary live sale exposes through the Purchase API. Purchase examples include refund/refunded flags, and custom-amount documentation shows a partial refund as a negative quantity/value. The Card Payments API separately documents full/partial refunds addressed to an original transaction ID, but that is not proof of a matching field or dedicated refund webhook in the passive Purchase/Pusher feed. Exact passive refund correlation and delivery still need validation. Sources: [Zettle FAQ](https://developer.zettle.com/docs/faq), [developer mode](https://developer.zettle.com/docs/payment-integrations/android-sdk/concepts/developer-mode), [purchase example](https://developer.zettle.com/docs/api/purchase/user-guides/fetch-purchases/fetch-a-purchase), [custom-amount refunds](https://developer.zettle.com/docs/api/purchase/concepts/custom-amounts), [Card Payments refunds](https://developer.zettle.com/docs/api/card-payments/create-refund).

### Fidel alongside merchant integrations

**Verified.** Fidel’s SDK can send card details directly to Fidel and return a card ID; this repository's verified transaction payloads expose it as nested `card.id`. Transactions are limited to enrolled cards and participating enabled locations. Fidel documents authorisation and clearing events, refund events and test transactions. Its refund matching to an original purchase uses card, merchant, location, amount and time, so even Fidel’s own refund relationship should be checked rather than presumed exact in every case. Commercial pricing, coverage for each intended UK merchant and permission terms need confirmation for the actual agreement.

## Minimal secure design

1. **Separate evidence from awards.** Store a minimal source event with provider, merchant account, provider payment or event ID, status, amount, currency and time. Verify webhook signatures where provided, acknowledge promptly, process asynchronously, and periodically reconcile from each provider’s transaction API. Use a uniqueness constraint on **provider plus merchant account plus provider payment ID** to stop repeat deliveries awarding twice. That key handles repeated reports of one provider payment; it does not merge a Fidel event with a Square, SumUp or Zettle event. Square explicitly documents repeat delivery.

2. **Keep identity proofs scoped.** Map Fidel card IDs to a consenting shopper. For Square, map a fingerprint to a shopper only after a confirmed first purchase, scoped to the seller and tested payment method. Do not store PAN or use last four as an identity key. Keep merchant OAuth tokens encrypted and access limited to the merchant connection. Revoke future matching when consent or merchant access is withdrawn. Fidel’s SDK route avoids routing raw card details through The Loyalty Loop’s servers.

3. **Choose an authoritative earning source per merchant and payment route.** If Fidel is active, credit its verified shopper events; use a merchant feed for reconciliation or merchant analytics only unless an exact, documented cross source reference is available. If Square alone is the approved automatic route, credit only its verified fingerprint matches. An equal amount, nearby timestamp, last four or similar merchant name is a **possible duplicate flag**, never proof that two reports are the same purchase. Where sources cannot be resolved, hold for review or use manual spend. No common transaction ID between Fidel and these POS providers has been established by the sources above.

4. **Maintain a spend ledger.** Record one positive award per accepted purchase and negative adjustments for refunds or chargebacks, tied to the original provider payment where its API supplies that relationship. Support multiple partial refunds without reducing credited spend below the net paid amount. For an ambiguous cross source refund, hold the adjustment for review rather than guessing. Retain raw events only for a defined reconciliation period, retain the minimum ledger needed for balances and disputes, and give shoppers clear separate choices for card linking, merchant specific matching and withdrawal. The precise UK retention period and lawful basis require a product specific privacy assessment.

## Pilot and effort

**Pilot Square first**, with one consenting UK Square merchant and one physical reader or terminal. It has the clearest documented path from a seller’s existing terminal sale to a payment webhook **and** a candidate repeat card identifier. Budget approximately **two to four weeks for one experienced developer** to build a narrow OAuth, webhook, ledger, consent and reconciliation pilot, assuming the existing Loyalty Loop backend and Fidel integration are usable. This is an engineering estimate, not a provider commitment. SumUp’s available Solo makes it the best **second device investigation**, particularly to verify that independently initiated sales appear in transaction history, but its documented last four does not justify automatic shopper matching.

Only a live, authorised device pilot can establish whether the intended UK hardware sends complete usable records, how quickly they arrive, whether a fingerprint stays consistent across repeated physical taps and separately enrolled wallets, how partial and delayed refunds appear, and whether Fidel and the merchant feed report the same purchase without a usable shared reference. Until those results and the applicable provider terms are confirmed, **manual spend is the safe default**.

## Coexistence and pilot

Use a separate ingestion adapter for Fidel and each merchant provider. Authenticate each webhook, store its provider event ID, and reconcile against the provider API. Deduplicate repeated events using the provider, merchant account and provider payment ID. Maintain a spend ledger with adjustments for verified partial or full refunds.

Choose **one authoritative earning source per merchant** initially. Splitting by payment route is safe only after proving those routes cannot overlap. Fidel card IDs and Square fingerprints are separate identifiers. A matching amount, time or last four is only a review clue, never proof that two feeds describe one purchase. If shopper identity or cross-source duplication cannot be resolved, hold the event for review; do not automatically add manual spend on top of an uncertain automatic award.

Pilot **Square first** at one consenting UK merchant with a real terminal. Estimated implementation is **two to four weeks for one experienced developer**, assuming the existing backend and Fidel work are usable. That estimate excludes merchant onboarding or contractual approval. Test the first card link, repeated physical card payments, phone wallets, delayed events and partial refunds before enabling automatic rewards. The available **SumUp Solo** is a useful second live investigation of ordinary Solo sales and transaction history, but its documented last four cannot support automatic shopper matching.
